Center — unbiased

The mean of the p̂ values settles near p. On average, p̂ hits the true proportion — it doesn’t systematically over- or underestimate.

Spread — bigger n, tighter

Quadruple n and the SD is cut in half: σp̂ = p(1−p)n. Try n = 25 vs. n = 100 with the same p and compare.

Shape — toward Normal

With small n or p near 0 / 1, the histogram is discrete and skewed. As np and n(1−p) grow past 10, it becomes smooth and bell-shaped (CLT).

Each sample is drawn with replacement from a population with proportion p of “successes.”  ·  Try this: set p = 0.10, n = 10 and take 1,000 samples — then raise n to 100 and watch the skew disappear.
